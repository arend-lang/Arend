### Algebra.Ring.Factor

Quotient ring construction: factoring a commutative ring by an ideal, with maximal ideals yielding fields and irreducible elements in Bezout rings yielding fields.

#### Factor Ring

- **`FactorRing`**: The quotient ring `R/I` for an ideal `I`, defined as `Quotient` by the relation `a ~ b ⟺ I (a - b)`. Instance of `CRing`.
- **`FactorRing.Type`**: Underlying type `Quotient {I.S} (\lam a b => I (a - b))`.
- **`FactorRing.inF`**: Canonical projection `I.S -> FactorRing I` mapping `a` to its equivalence class.
- **`FactorRing.fequiv`**: From `I (a - b)`, derive `inF a = inF b` in the quotient.
- **`FactorRing.fequiv0`**: From `I a`, derive `inF a = inF 0`.
- **`FactorRing.unfequiv`**: Inverse of `fequiv`: from `inF a = inF b`, recover `I (a - b)`.
- **`FactorRing.unfequiv0`**: From `inF a = inF 0`, recover `I a`.

#### Universal Property

- **`factorHom`**: The canonical ring homomorphism `R -> R/I` given by `inF`.
- **`factor-lift`**: Universal property of the quotient: a ring homomorphism `f : R -> S` that vanishes on `I` (i.e., `I a -> f a = 0`) factors uniquely through `R/I -> S`.

#### Factor Field for Maximal Ideals

- **`FactorField`**: For a maximal ideal `I`, `R/I` is a `DiscreteField`. Uses `Ideal.IsMaximal` to ensure `0 ≠ 1` and that every nonzero element has an inverse.
- **`FactorField.conv`**: Converse: if `R/I` is a discrete field (i.e., `0 ≠ 1` and every element is zero or invertible), then `I` is maximal.

#### Factor Field for Irreducibles in Bezout Rings

- **`FactorIrrField`**: For a Bezout ring `R` and an irreducible element `p`, the quotient `R/(p)` (factoring by the principal ideal generated by `p`) is a `DiscreteField`. Uses Bezout coefficients combined with irreducibility to decide divisibility or invert non-divisible elements.
- **`FactorIrrField.trivial_Inv`**: `0 = 1` in `R/(p)` iff `p` is invertible in `R`.
- **`FactorIrrField.zeroOrInv-char`**: Characterizes when every element of `R/(p)` is zero or invertible: equivalent to every `a : R` either being divisible by `p` or having a Bezout combination `s * a + t * p = 1`.
- **`FactorIrrField.conv`**: Converse: if multiplication by `p` is cancellative and `R/(p)` is a discrete field, then `p` is prime.
