### Algebra.Ring.ZeroDimensional

Characterizations and consequences of zero-dimensional commutative rings, von Neumann regular rings, and their relationship to fields.

A commutative ring is zero-dimensional when every prime ideal is maximal, which classically corresponds to Krull dimension zero. This module provides constructive equivalences between zero-dimensionality and conditions involving idempotent multiples of powers (`a^k` divides and is divided by some idempotent `b`), connects this notion to von Neumann regularity (which adds the reduced condition), and shows that local zero-dimensional rings collapse to fields. The von Neumann regular characterization further links to ideal-theoretic properties (idempotent ideals, distributivity of meet and product), and Bézout structure follows automatically.

#### Zero-Dimensional Rings

- **`zeroDimensional-char`**: TFAE characterization of zero-dimensionality for a commutative ring `R`: (1) `R.IsZeroDimensional`, (2) `R.Dim<= 0`, (3) for every `a : R`, there exist `b : R`, `k : Nat` with `k /= 0`, `b` idempotent, and mutual divisibility between `pow a k` and `b`.
- **`zeroDimensional-char.aux`**: Helper lemma showing that if `x = x * c`, then `x = x * pow c n` for any `n`.
- **`zeroDimensional_local-char`**: TFAE for local-style zero-dimensional rings: (1) zero-dimensional plus every `a` or `a + 1` is invertible, (2) zero-dimensional plus connected, (3) every `a` is either invertible or nilpotent.

#### Von Neumann Regular Rings

- **`vonNeumann_idempotent`**: For a von Neumann regular `R` and any `a : R`, produces an idempotent `u` with `LDiv a u` and `a = u * a` — every element has an associated idempotent generator.
- **`vonNeumann-char`**: TFAE characterization of von Neumann regularity: (1) `R.IsVonNeumannRegular`, (2) zero-dimensional and reduced, (3) every finitely generated ideal is generated by a single idempotent, (4) every ideal is idempotent under the ideal monoid product, (5) ideal meet equals ideal product for all pairs.
- **`vonNeumann_Bezout`**: Every von Neumann regular ring is Bézout (finitely generated ideals are principal).

#### Connection to Fields

- **`zeroDim->inv`**: In an impotent commutative ring, zero-dimensionality implies every element is either zero or invertible.
- **`zeroDim->field`**: Constructs a `DiscreteField` structure on a zero-dimensional impotent commutative ring, witnessing `zro/=ide` and `eitherZeroOrInv`.
